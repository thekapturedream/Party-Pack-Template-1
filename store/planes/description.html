Ten paper planes that actually fly, with every fold drawn out and the design already printed on the sheet you fold.

Print a template, fold along the printed lines, and the plane comes out right the first time — wearing a livery that lands exactly where it should. No glue, no scissors, no tape. Ordinary printer paper.

What makes this different from a page of paper plane pictures

  • The fold lines are printed on the template. A six-year-old can match a crease to a line instead of guessing where the middle is.
  • Every fold is drawn, in order, at the same size. You can see the model shrinking as you go, which is the strongest cue there is that you are on the right step.
  • Nothing is printed on the part of the sheet that folds away. The design only goes where it will still be visible, so a template costs about half the ink you would expect.
  • Ten different flights. One for distance, one for hang time, one to throw through a hoop, one that comes back to you. No two of them do the same thing in the air.

  • 6. Bulldog — blunt nose, safe to throw indoors

  • 8. Boomerang — throw it away, it comes back
  • 9. Kestrel — locked nose, narrow wing, flies where you point it
  • 10. Moth — almost all wing, barely falls

Printing

  • A4 and US Letter in the same download. Each plane is drawn separately for each size, so the printed fold lines land in the right place on both.
  • Print at 100% or actual size, never “fit to page”. Corner ticks on every template prove your printer did not shrink the page.
  • Ordinary printer paper. Not card — card will not fly.
  • Black and white works throughout. Nothing needs colour to be understood.
